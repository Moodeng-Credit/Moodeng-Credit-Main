Part 0 · The evidence

What borrowers actually do

Before deciding what the screens should look like, we looked at how people use them today: PostHog (clicks, rage and dead clicks, page views, last 90 days) and our loans database. The numbers are small, 30 funded loans from 14 borrowers and 35 people who opened Repay, so read them as strong signals, not statistics.

4 of 5late repayments were paid on the due date itself, in the evening in Manila. The cut-off was 8:00 AM.
11 of 20loans were repaid on the last day. 13 of 20 between 5 PM and midnight.
2 of 20loans were paid in more than one go. Almost everyone pays it all at once.
11 of 35people opened Repay 10+ times. One borrower opened it 30 times in a single day.
11 of 22people who opened cash out tapped "I'll do this later".
Biggest issue

People don't know when their loan is due

18 of 20 repaid loans were due at 8:00 AM Manila (stored as midnight UTC). The app says "Due Sep 20", so people treat the whole day as theirs. All 4 same-day "late" payments came that evening: 6:05 PM, 8:24 PM, 10:03 PM and 11:30 PM. Late means no Pandesal and a broken streak, for paying on the day they thought was fine.

Support

People struggle in silence

In 90 days, one support chat mentioned a loan problem. Nobody asked how to repay. When the screen confuses them, they keep tapping, leave, or pay late. They don't ask.

Source: support chat conversations

What this means for the design

  1. Make the deadline impossible to misread. "Due today, 8:00 AM" in local time with a countdown, red on the last day, and a reminder the evening before. Product call for us: move the due time to 11:59 PM Manila, which matches how people actually behave.
  2. Design for the last-minute, evening payer. Most people pay at night on the final day. From opening the app to paid should take one screen and one tap when the wallet has enough.
  3. Make "Pay it all" the default. 18 of 20 paid in one go. Paying part is a small secondary option, not four equal chips.
  4. Repay doubles as a status page. At a glance: what's due, by when, whether a top-up has arrived, and "You're all set" for people with nothing to pay.
  5. Only make tappable things look tappable. Amounts and labels that people keep tapping should either do something or stop looking like buttons.
  6. Cash out: lead with Coins.ph and Max pre-filled, and give "I'll do this later" a reminder instead of a dead end.
Part 1 · The product

How Moodeng works

Everything the screens have to get right. These are the real rules in the app today.

Who borrows

  • People in the Philippines, on their phones, often inside the Facebook or Messenger browser.
  • Most are new to crypto. The loan is in USDC, a digital dollar.
  • Loans are small: from $15 up to $140 as trust grows.
  • Built in English first. Filipino comes later and runs about 30% longer.

The loan

  • The borrower picks their own due date, often payday, up to 120 days out.
  • One due date. No monthly installments.
  • They can pay part of it any time before the due date. Never more than they owe.
  • Late: the account is restricted until the loan is fully paid.

The wallet

  • New borrowers get a Moodeng Instant Wallet built into the app automatically. Some earlier borrowers still use an outside wallet (Base Account by Coinbase); new users won't.
  • Money in: the loan lands here. To repay, they top it up by buying USDC in Coins.ph, GCrypto, PDAX or Binance and sending it on the Base network (Binance only outside the Philippines).
  • Money out: cash out to one of those apps (or Moneybees), then sell for pesos. The first cash-out needs a quick face check.

The game

  • +10 Pandesal for every loan fully repaid on time. Paying part earns nothing until the loan is done.
  • Streak bonus for on-time loans in a row: 3 → +15, 5 → +25, 10 → +50. A late loan resets the streak; nothing is taken away.
  • Moodeng grows with Pandesal: Rookie → Rising (60) → Prime (200) → Apex (400). Each new tier unlocks a GrabFood voucher: ₱50, ₱100, ₱150. The first on-time repayment also gets ₱50.
  • Credit Level: borrow your full limit and repay it in full, and your limit goes up: $15 → $20 → $40 → $60 … $140. Paying late doesn't block this.

Part 4 · Simplify

How to make it simpler

Simple doesn't mean empty. It means each screen answers one question, loud and clear, and everything else steps back.

1

One job per screen

Use the flow above. If something doesn't help with this step's job, remove it or make it a small link.

2

One number, in words

"Left to pay $81" at the top, once. Never the same amount five times with no label.

3

Pay it all is the default

18 of 20 loans were paid in one go. "Pay $81.00" is the main action; paying part is a small link, not four equal chips.

4

The button is always visible

Pinned above the bottom menu, solid purple, saying exactly what happens: "Pay $81.00".

5

Space instead of boxes

Group with spacing and type size. Use a card only for something you tap.

6

Crypto words only where needed

"USDC" and "Base" belong on the send step. Everywhere else: dollars, your wallet, your loan.

Cut list from the current designs

Payment schedule button and sheet → a small History link

"Watch how to repay" and "Need help?" cards → two text links

Wallet card → one line above the button: "Enough to pay ✓" or "$63 short"

Input box inside a card inside the page → one big editable amount

Four equal chips (25 / 50 / 75 / Full) → "Pay it all" by default, "Pay part" as a link

Red warning boxes → calm notes; red only when something went wrong

"Check again" button → the app checks by itself

GrabFood banner while paying → show it after, on the celebration

Part 1 · What changed

New users now get a Moodeng Instant Wallet

The first brief was written around outside crypto wallets (Base Account by Coinbase). Since then, every new user gets an Instant Wallet built into Moodeng, and that's the path from now on. Some existing borrowers still use an outside wallet (Base Account), so the screens should still work for them, but design for the Instant Wallet first.

What this changes in the designs

  • New users never connect a wallet; it's created for them. Show the wallet a person has instead of a "Connect Wallet" card.
  • No approval pop-up when paying from the Instant Wallet. Tapping Pay sends the money straight away, so the confirm step is the last chance to stop.
  • "Pay with" should name the wallet they use: Moodeng wallet for most, not "USDC (Base)".
  • The first cash-out asks for a quick face check (anti-fraud). It needs screens.
  • Instant Wallet users can't disconnect or switch the wallet themselves.

What stays the same

  • The wallet still holds USDC on the Base network.
  • Topping up still means sending USDC from Coins.ph, GCrypto, PDAX and similar apps to the wallet address. The Base network warning stays.
  • It works inside the Facebook and Messenger browser.
Design review

What's better, and what still needs work

A lot of this is better than the live app. Each problem below is checked against what borrowers actually do (Part 0). Simple is the right direction for people new to crypto using the app inside Facebook. But a simple screen has to look more finished, because there's less on it to hide behind. Too many screens still read as the old app restyled with dashboard cards: not fun, not cute, not a game like the dashboard, and with a lot of small mistakes.

Severity: Big breaks the main goal of the brief · Medium confuses people or contradicts the product · Small polish.

Better than before

  • The Pay button sits next to the amount. It's no longer a circle hidden in the bottom menu, the #1 problem in the brief.
  • Loan repaid is a real celebration: the hippo, points and the Credit Level bar. This is the bar for every other screen.
  • Overdue is clear without being scary: red card, a worried hippo and one message.
  • Topping up is split into steps (Add funds → Send → Checking) instead of one long page.
  • A confirm step before paying, which matters now that the Instant Wallet has no approval pop-up.
  • The slider is gone. Chips plus an editable amount is enough.
  • The empty screen turns "no loan" into a clear next step. It only needs the wallet card updated.
  • Her notes next to each frame explain the trade-offs, which makes reviewing easy.

Still needs work: the overall approach

01

It isn't fun, cute or gamified

Big
What's wrong
The brief asked for "cute and warm: Moodeng the hippo, rewards, a real celebration at the end." The dashboard is already a game: your Moodeng grows from Rookie to Rising, Prime and Apex as you feed it Pandesal, and there are weekly streaks and GrabFood vouchers. Repaying is where borrowers earn most of that. In the new screens it shrinks to a small yellow "+10Pandesal" chip and a thin progress bar. Nothing reacts when you pick an amount, nothing shows what paying will unlock, and the only joy comes after it's over. Paying off a loan is the best thing a borrower can do in Moodeng, and right now it feels like a bank form in purple.
Where
Every repay screen, most of all the loan page, the confirm sheet and the success screens.
Ideas
  • Show the prize before they pay. On the loan page: "+10 on time, +15 streak bonus, unlocks Rising and a ₱50 GrabFood voucher", with the next-tier hippo next to the button. If this loan was for their full limit, also show the new limit they'll get.
  • Make the amount react. Paying it all shows the full prize. Paying part shows what's still to win, since Pandesal only comes when the loan is fully repaid on time.
  • Feed the hippo. On success, the Pandesal flies into the hippo and the tier bar fills, using the dashboard's own "feeding" idea, instead of a static "+10" card.
  • A real celebration. Motion on the repaid screen (coins, confetti, the tier or level reveal) and a share card ("I paid off my loan on time") using the dashboard's existing Facebook and WhatsApp share.
  • Show the streak. "2 on time in a row. One more for +15." The streak bonuses (3, 5, 10 in a row) already exist and nobody sees them.
  • Emotion instead of alarms. On an overdue loan, a worried hippo that cheers up as they pay, instead of red boxes.

Must keep: only show rewards we actually give (see "The game" in Part 1). New reward ideas are welcome, but label them as proposals so we can decide.

02

It still looks AI-made, and there's too much on each screen

Big
What's wrong
We asked for a redesign because the old repay screens were made by AI and felt generic. The new ones have the same tells:
  • Every element sits in a rounded white card with the same soft shadow.
  • Soft purple gradient buttons on white, under a pale lavender gradient header.
  • Icon-in-a-circle rows with a chevron ("Watch how to repay", "Need help?").
  • Two-tone money ("$102.60") and a red alert box with a warning triangle.
On top of that, there's too much on each screen. The Option 3 loan page shows a loan card, a schedule button, a question, an amount box inside a card, four chips, a button, a date row, a wallet card and the bottom menu, all at once.
Our part
The first brief told her to reuse the dashboard's purple gradient buttons and card style, so some of this came from us. She can change those if it makes the screens feel more like Moodeng.
Fix
Cut each screen to what that step needs, about 3 or 4 things. Use spacing and type size for hierarchy instead of a card around everything. Swap generic patterns for Moodeng's own (the hippo, Pandesal, the tiers). The Loan repaid screen already gets this right.
03

It's a restyle, not a redesign

Big

The three options are small layout changes to the same screen, not three different ideas: Option 2 is Option 1 plus one button. Option 3, a separate page for each loan, is the one real change in structure, and it\'s the one we\'re going with. We asked for repaying to feel like the dashboard, meaning simpler, cuter and friendlier, not only matching colors.

Fix

04

Every screen is a stack of equal white cards

Big
What's wrong
Loans, wallet, amount, help links and banners all get the same white rounded card with the same shadow. Nothing stands out, so the eye has no starting point. The brief's main ask was one clear thing to do on each screen.
Where

Fix
One focal point per screen: the amount owed, big, near the top. Make secondary items (help, video, banner) lighter: plain rows or links, not cards.
05

The Repay button is still below the fold

Big

Why it's hard to get wrong

  1. One thing at a time. The big button is always the next step, and steps unlock in order. You can't open Coins.ph before copying your address.
  2. It uses Coins.ph's words, not ours. "Transfer → Send Crypto → USDC" is what they'll actually see on the other app's screens.
  3. The network is a step, not a warning. "Pick Base", with the Base logo, at the exact moment Coins.ph asks. Calm blue instead of a red error box.
  4. A whole number to type: 63. If the amount has cents, we round up, so nobody has to remember cents or ends up a few cents short.
  5. A simple address check: "ends in 9D4e", big and bold, instead of a 42-character address.
  6. Waiting happens right here. Anna's waiting hippo moves onto this screen, with a live status that moves on by itself when the money lands. No separate page and no "Check again" button.
  7. Done means paying. When the money arrives, the button becomes "Pay $81.00".
