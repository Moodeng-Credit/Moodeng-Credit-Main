Is this stock cheap? Four numbers tell you.

P/E, forward P/E, free cash flow yield and PEG, explained in plain English with real numbers for 18 stocks you know. Every stock is measured against the safest bet in the world: a US government bond.

Our top 3 by the numbers

Which stocks pass all our tests?

We ran 18 well-known stocks through four tests. Each test asks the same thing: does this stock pay you more than the 5.24% bond, or is its growth cheap for what you pay?

  1. 1. Earnings yield over 5.24%Last year's profit ÷ price
  2. 2. Forward earnings yield over 5.24%Next year's expected profit ÷ price
  3. 3. FCF yield over 5.24%Real cash made ÷ company value
  4. 4. PEG under 1.5Price is fair for the growth expected

    Close calls: General Motors also passes 3 tests, but its cheap forward numbers depend on profit jumping about seven-fold next year. We break ties by picking the stock that relies least on forecasts. JPMorgan passes every test we can run, but banks don't report free cash flow the way other companies do, so we can't score it fully.

    Cheap on paper isn't always cheap

    A low number can mean a bargain, or a business the market thinks is shrinking. Before you trust any ratio, compare the stock with others in the same industry and ask why it is cheap. Banks, oil companies and carmakers usually trade at lower P/Es than software companies, and that is normal.

    This page is education, not advice to buy or sell. Moodeng Credit is not an investment adviser, and our scorecard is a simple teaching tool, not a recommendation for your situation. Stocks can lose value. Check the numbers yourself before you invest, and never invest money you've borrowed or need soon.
