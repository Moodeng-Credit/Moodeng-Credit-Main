How risk works

The honest part.

Borrowers get no hidden charges. You get no hidden risk.

Repayment isn’t guaranteed. If a borrower doesn’t pay, you can lose what you lent. No insurance fund. A real loan to a real person.
That’s why everyone starts at $15. Limits only grow through repayments that actually happened. Never fund more than you’d be okay losing.
On a default: account frozen, miss on the record every future lender sees, no starting over with a fresh account. What never happens: harassment.
This is helping, with your money back at the end. Not a savings account. The extra few dollars are a thank-you.

Common questions

Questions lenders ask us.

Is this charity or investing?

Neither. It’s person-to-person lending: you help someone through a tight week, and they pay back a little more than they borrowed. If you want to grow savings, this isn’t it. If you want your money to mean something and come back, it is.

What’s the track record?

As of July 2026: 102 verified borrowers, 94% repaid on time, every repayment on a public record. Last month’s loans were all repaid — most a day or two before deadline. We’re early; we’d rather show small real numbers than big invented ones.

What do I actually earn?

One flat payback the borrower sets upfront — fund $15, get back $18 is typical. No APR, no compounding: what you see when you fund is exactly what comes back.

What happens if someone doesn’t repay?

You can lose what you lent — we won’t pretend otherwise. Every borrower is verified as one real person (no fresh accounts after a default), everyone starts at $15, and a miss freezes their borrowing and goes on their record. What we don’t do: harass anyone.

How do I put money in?

With a debit card, right in the app — or digital dollars you already have. Funding a $15 request takes about a minute.

When do I get paid back?

On or before the due date the borrower set — you see the exact date before you fund. In practice most repayments land on payday, often a day or two early.

How do I choose well?

Read the reason, then the record. Repaid loans and on-time history show you how someone behaves. First-timers have no record yet — that’s what the $15 cap is for.
